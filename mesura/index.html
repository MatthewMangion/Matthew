mesura.ai · Brand explorations · Prototypes

One mark, read seven ways.

Mesura’s mark is an eight-point star with an opening cut into it. Read one way it is a sun. Read others, it is a seal, a tile, a technical drawing, a dial, a chart or an aperture. Each reading below is built out into a working design system with its own colour, type, shape, motion and components.

Six things never change: the mark, the name, pine and ochre, serif numerals, the ranked stat colours and a plain voice.

01 Almanac · Antiquity · the mark as a sun

Readiness grows in rings.

The oldest measures are of things that grow: the height of the Nile in flood, the length of a noon shadow, the rings in a felled trunk. They were read slowly and taken again each season. Almanac treats AI readiness the same way, as something an organisation grows into and measures every quarter. The mark becomes the sun it already half resembles.

02 Hallmark · 1300 onwards · the mark as a seal

Measured, then marked.

Since 1300, silver in England has been tested and struck with a hallmark before sale. The mark says it has been measured and is what it claims to be. Hallmark gives Mesura’s report the same standing. The eight-point mark becomes the punch, struck into every certificate of readiness.

03 Madum · c. 1900 · the mark as a tile

Madum

A floor is a measure you can walk on.

Walk into an older Maltese townhouse and the floor is a grid of patterned cement tiles, madum, each one the same size, repeated until the room is full. The eight-point star has belonged to that Mediterranean pattern language for centuries. Madum builds Mesura from the tile up. Every layout sits on a grid of equal units, every pattern is made from the mark, and a floor can carry data.

    04 Tolerance · 1940s · the mark as a drawing

    Every dimension needs a tolerance. ± how far it may vary and still be right

    An engineer never draws a part without saying how far it may vary and still be right. Tolerance is a design system drawn the way parts are drawn. Every component carries its own dimensions, so it can be checked the way a part is checked. The logo gets a construction sheet, and the interface is Mesura measuring itself.

    05 Dial · 1960s · the mark as a knob

Dial imagines Mesura as an object in the tradition of mid-century industrial design. Honest materials, one coloured key, one knob. The opening in the mark finally does what it always looked like it should. It points.

    06 Datum · 2010s · the mark as a chart

    Every point is a measurement.

    Dataism, a term popularised by the historian Yuval Noah Harari, describes a world that values anything by the data it produces. Datum takes the idea at its word, with Mesura’s restraint. Every element in the system is a measurement, the logo included. The mark has eight points, so here they are eight dimensions, drawn to scale from each organisation’s results. The opening turns to face the dimension with the most room to grow.

    Calibrated29.09.2026

    Since May 2019, every SI unit has been defined by a fixed constant of nature. The kilogram no longer depends on a metal cylinder kept in a vault outside Paris. It can be reproduced anywhere, with the right instrument. Constant imagines Mesura as that instrument: calibrated, luminous and exact. The mark becomes an aperture. Readiness is read clockwise from the opening, and light passes through it.
